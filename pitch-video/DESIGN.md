# Ghurni — Before Landfall

## Style Prompt
A cinematic documentary pitch inspired by the supplied Mission Control Jr film: urgent opening montage, condensed monumental titles, warm rust intertitles, restrained monospaced technical labels, breathing holds, and a quiet final appeal. The actual Ghurni application and archived forecast geometry are the visual evidence. Preserve the map's original data colours. Alternate large editorial statements with generous product captures; avoid a slide-deck rhythm.

## Colors
- Ink `#071b20`: ocean, negative space, caption band.
- Paper `#f5ead8`: primary type.
- Rust `#aa4825`: opening title field and transitions.
- Amber `#ffb078`: emphasis and archival forecast tracks.
- Teal `#80c7ba`: supporting diagram geometry.

## Typography
Anton: condensed display voice, headlines 110–220px.
IBM Plex Mono: analytical voice, captions 35px, labels 23–30px.
Noto Sans Bengali: the Ghurni wordmark only, 96px.
Fonts and GSAP are bundled locally; rendering never needs a font CDN.

## Layout
1920 × 1080, 30 fps, exactly 180 seconds. Main content within 100px side margins. Product frames occupy the right two thirds or the full cinematic field. Lower caption band remains clear. Real application captures retain embedded source attribution; expanded source credits accompany them.

## Motion
12-frame primary dissolves; rust wipe at act changes. Opening image fragments use 0.15-second dissolves, angular framing and rapid rhythm. Slow pans alternate with path drawing, count reveals, and stillness. All animation uses the paused, seekable GSAP timeline. No live network maps or runtime app dependency.

## What NOT to Do
- Do not imply that the 2024 satellite mosaic is live storm imagery.
- Do not invent cyclone observations, operational deployments, or AI features.
- Do not reuse the reference music or space/game footage.
- Do not recolour probability maps or cover their legend with editorial claims.
- Do not use tiny pitch-deck paragraphs or a fake clicking cursor.
